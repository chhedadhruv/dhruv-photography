The camera was new enough that I was still taking it places to find out what it did. This
was one of those places — The Pizza Bakery in HSR Layout, Bengaluru, an evening in April,
friends, and no occasion beyond dinner. I brought it on purpose. These are some of the
first frames I got out of it.

By the time I started shooting, the room was emptying out. That turns out to matter more
than anything I did with the settings.

## What a kit lens gives you in a dark room

The RF-S 18-45mm opens to f/4.5 at the wide end and f/6.3 at the long end. In a restaurant
lit by one warm bulb over each table, that leaves you two levers and both of them cost
something.

All four frames are ISO 6400. Three of them are between 1/25s and 1/30s, which is about
the floor for hand-holding this lens before the frame starts to smear. The oven shot is
1/60s at 45mm, and only because the fire was doing the lighting.

There is no version of this evening where I get clean files. The choice was grain or
nothing, and grain photographs a room better than a flash does.

## The oven

The photograph I like most is the one I had the least to do with. It was shot from our
table, across the room, at the long end of the zoom — I did not ask him, and he did not
look up. The oven lit him and everything above the counter went black on its own.

That is the whole trick, and it is not a trick: find the one bright thing in a dark room
and let the rest go.

## The burrata

The pizza was burrata on a wood-fired sourdough base, and the reason it looks like it does
is the way the chef put the cheese on — torn and spread by hand, right at the end. It made
the whole thing look better than a neater version of it would have.

I shot it from directly above, uncut, before anyone touched it. The drink from an earlier
frame is still sitting in the corner of the shot, refusing to get out of the way.

## What the photographs missed

Four frames, twelve minutes, and the food was genuinely good. But what I actually remember
from that evening is my friends and how much we laughed, and there is no frame here that
has any of that in it.

Which is probably the most useful thing I learned that night. The camera got the oven and
the burrata. It was never going to get the rest.
